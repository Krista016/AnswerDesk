Cat Suddenly Limping: Sprain vs Fracture vs Infected Bite Abscess Triage

Written & Audited by Dr. Amanda Hayes, DVM Licensed Emergency Veterinarian • AnswerDesk Vetted Expert • Updated November 2026
⚡ Quick Expert Answer • Veterinary & Pet Urgent Care

Examine paw pads for foreign bodies, palpate for warm swollen abscesses, and check joint range of motion.

Pre-Fix Diagnostic Checklist

  • ✓Inspect paw pads and between toes for thorns, splinters, or torn claw sheaths
  • ✓Check for localized heat, swelling, and puncture marks (cat fight abscess)
  • ✓Restrict cat to small room/carrier to prevent jumping and further joint damage
  • ✓Write down the exact error message, flashing LED pattern, or fault code displayed before restarting the device.

Diagnostic & Troubleshooting Protocol

Examine paw pads for foreign bodies, palpate for warm swollen abscesses, and check joint range of motion.

Step 1
Inspect Paw Pads and Claws

Ingrown claws curving back into paw pads, torn dewclaws, or embedded glass splinters are the most frequent causes of sudden non-weight-bearing lameness.

Gently spread toes with good lighting
Step 2
Check for Puncture Wounds / Abscesses

Cat bite punctures heal over quickly on the surface, trapping anaerobic bacteria underneath that form a painful, fever-inducing abscess within 48 hours.

Gently feel along the limping leg for localized warmth, soft swelling, and tenderness
Step 3
Test for Bone Fractures

Complete refusal to put any weight on limb, visible bone deformity, or audible crying when touched requires immediate emergency splinting and radiographs.

Observe whether cat allows any weight on limb
Expert Caution / Safety Note: Never give human pain medications (Tylenol/Acetaminophen, Advil/Ibuprofen, Aspirin) to cats; a single Tylenol tablet causes fatal toxic methemoglobinemia.
