Official Standard Operating Procedure

Expert Verification & Enrollment

How Our Multi-Stage Expert Vetting Process Works • Complete 18-Step Standard Operating Procedure

🌟 Foundational Philosophy

Become a Verified Expert

Our platform is built around a simple principle:

💎 Core Standard of Trust

People seeking professional advice should be connected with professionals whose identity, qualifications, relevant expertise, and ongoing quality have been appropriately verified.

We therefore use a multi-stage verification system designed to verify both who you are and what you are qualified to advise on.

Our process is designed to be rigorous enough to protect customers and the integrity of the platform, while remaining straightforward for legitimate, qualified professionals.

Our Three-Phase Verification System

Our expert verification process comprises three primary pillars:

📜
Phase 1 — Professional Auth
"Are you genuinely qualified to practice your profession?"
Identity, education, primary licensing, background screening.
🎯
Phase 2 — Specialty Scope
"Are you experienced in the specific areas you advise on?"
Granular taxonomy, board credentials, clinical reasoning, safety.
⭐
Phase 3 — Ongoing Quality
"Do you consistently provide safe, useful, professional answers?"
Probation period, peer review audits, automated monitoring.
⚖️
Selective Authorization
Crucial Rule: Passing one phase does not automatically mean that an expert is verified for every type of question.
1
Phase 1: Professional Verification

"Are you genuinely qualified to practice your profession?"

Step 01

1. Identity Verification

We need to establish that you are the person applying for the expert account.

Depending on your country and profession, you may be asked to provide:

  • Legal name
  • Date of birth
  • Current contact information
  • Government-issued identification (Passport, Driver's License, National ID)
  • Selfie or real-time liveness biometric verification
  • Other information necessary to confirm your identity

Identity information may be checked using independent verification services.

Why do we verify identity?

Professional credentials are meaningful only when they can be reliably connected to the person holding the account. We do not permit an individual to use another person's license, qualification, or professional identity.

Step 02

2. Education & Qualification Verification

Where relevant to your profession, we may verify:

  • Degree or diploma
  • Professional qualification
  • University or educational institution
  • Graduation or qualification date
  • Professional postgraduate training
  • Certifications
  • Additional recognized qualifications

We may ask you to provide supporting documents during enrollment. Where practical, important credentials may be independently verified with the issuing institution, professional body, certification organization, or another reliable source.

Important: Uploading a certificate does not necessarily mean that the certificate itself is sufficient for approval. We may independently verify the information provided.

Step 03

3. Professional License & Registration Verification

For regulated professions, maintaining an appropriate active professional license or registration may be mandatory.

Regulated professions include:

  • Veterinary medicine
  • Medicine & Healthcare
  • Dentistry
  • Law
  • Accounting & Tax Practice
  • Certain engineering professions
  • Other regulated professional services

We may verify: License or registration number, Issuing authority, Jurisdiction, Current status, Expiration date, Professional standing, and Relevant disciplinary or regulatory information.

Whenever practical, verification is performed using an official professional or regulatory source rather than relying solely on documents supplied by the applicant.

Your Responsibility: You must provide accurate and current information regarding your professional authorization. If your license expires, is suspended, restricted, revoked, or materially changes, you must notify us promptly where required by our policies.
Step 04

4. Professional Experience Verification

Qualifications alone do not always establish practical expertise. Depending on the category, we may verify your professional experience.

You may be asked to provide:

  • Current or previous employers
  • Practice or business information
  • Job titles and roles
  • Dates of employment
  • Areas of professional practice
  • Years of active experience
  • Relevant professional references
  • Other reasonable evidence of professional experience

Experience requirements can vary by profession, specialty, jurisdiction, and category.

Why this matters: An expert may be fully qualified in a profession while having limited experience in a particular specialty. Our system therefore distinguishes between professional qualification and specific professional expertise.

Step 05

5. Background & Professional Standing Review

Where legally permitted and appropriate, we may conduct background or professional-standing checks.

These may include:

  • Identity consistency cross-checks
  • Professional disciplinary history
  • License restrictions, suspensions, or revocations
  • Regulatory actions or consent decrees
  • Other relevant publicly available professional records
  • Background information appropriate to the category and jurisdiction

A potential record or match does not necessarily mean automatic rejection. Where appropriate, we conduct a manual review to determine whether the information is relevant, accurate, current, and material to the expert category.

2
Phase 2: Expertise & Specialty Verification

Your Professional Qualification Is Not Your Entire Expertise

Being a qualified professional does not automatically mean that you are an expert in every area within that profession.

For this reason, our platform uses a structured expertise system. Instead of simply identifying an expert as a generic "Veterinarian", we may identify their verified expertise more specifically as:

• Veterinary → Dogs → Dermatology • Veterinary → Cats → Internal Medicine • Law → Immigration → Employment-Based Immigration • Technology → Cybersecurity → Cloud Security

The exact categories and specialties available depend on the profession.

Step 06

6. Select Your Areas of Expertise

During enrollment, you will select the areas in which you are qualified and experienced to provide advice. Our expertise structure follows a hierarchical mapping:

Profession / Domain → Specialty → Sub-specialty

For example, in Veterinary: Dogs, Cats, Birds, Reptiles, Horses, Large Animals.
And within those areas: Internal Medicine, Dermatology, Surgery, Emergency Care, Dentistry, Nutrition, Neurology, Cardiology, Oncology, and other specialties.

Step 07

7. Specialty Experience Verification

Selecting a specialty does not automatically make that specialty verified. If you claim Veterinary Dermatology, we evaluate whether your professional history reasonably supports that claim.

Evidence may include: Relevant employment history, Clinical/practice experience, Specialist training or fellowships, Recognized certifications, Professional memberships, Continuing education, and documented previous responsibilities.

Step 08

8. Specialist Credential Verification

Some professions have formally recognized specialist qualifications or board certifications. Where applicable, we verify those credentials separately.

Professional qualification: Licensed Veterinarian — Verified
Specialty qualification: Recognized Veterinary Dermatology Specialist Board Credential — Verified

These are not necessarily the same thing. We will not represent an expert as a formally recognized specialist unless the relevant specialist credential has been independently verified.

Experience vs. Formal Specialist Status: An expert may have extensive experience in an area without holding a formal specialist credential. In such cases, we describe the expertise based on verified experience rather than using a protected specialist designation.

Step 09

9. Specialty Knowledge Assessment

Depending on the category, experts may be required to complete an assessment relating specifically to the expertise they want to offer.

The assessment evaluates:

  • Professional knowledge & diagnostic accuracy
  • Practical clinical/technical reasoning
  • Understanding of common scenarios & edge cases
  • Appropriate questioning to gather critical details
  • Recognition of important risks and red flags
  • Appropriate escalation protocols
  • Professional judgment under uncertainty
  • Understanding the boundaries and limitations of remote advice

Important: Passing a general professional assessment does not qualify an expert for every specialty. Where specialty assessments are used, they are linked to the specific area of expertise being claimed.

Step 10

10. Safety Assessment

For categories where incorrect advice could cause significant harm (such as medical triage, pet health, structural engineering, or urgent legal deadlines), we conduct a dedicated safety assessment.

The assessment evaluates whether an expert reliably recognizes:

  • Emergencies and acute life/health risks
  • High-risk situations requiring immediate in-person assistance (e.g. 911, ER, in-person vet clinic)
  • Circumstances where providing a definitive answer remotely would be inappropriate or unsafe
  • Situations outside the expert's verified scope

Safety is treated separately from general knowledge. An expert may have excellent technical knowledge but still be required to demonstrate safety judgment before approval.

Our 4-Level Expertise Hierarchy

To avoid overstating an expert's qualifications, our platform distinguishes between four distinct levels of verified authorization:

Level 1

Professionally Verified

Identity and relevant primary professional credentials independently verified.

Example: Verified Veterinarian

Level 2

Verified Experience

Demonstrated documented practical professional experience in a specific sub-domain.

Example: Verified Dog Veterinary Experience

Level 3

Verified Specialty Expertise

Passed applicable specialty examinations and clinical validation standards.

Example: Verified Veterinary Dermatology Expertise

Level 4

Recognized Specialist

Formally recognized specialty board certification verified with the accrediting college.

Example: Board-Certified Recognized Specialist

3
Phase 3: Platform Quality Verification & Monitoring

Approval Is Not the End of Verification

A professional can have legitimate credentials and still provide poor answers. For this reason, our verification process continues throughout an expert's tenure on AnswerDesk. We evaluate actual performance on the platform.

Step 11

11. New Expert Probation

Newly approved experts begin with a monitored probation period. During this period, our senior audit team closely evaluates their initial consultations.

Depending on the category and risk tier, the probation period involves a defined volume of answers (e.g. initial quality review: first 30–50 answers) before unmonitored privileges are granted.

Step 12

12. Answer Quality Monitoring

We evaluate answers against ten foundational criteria:

1. Accuracy
2. Relevance
3. Completeness
4. Professional Reasoning
5. Communication Clarity
6. Appropriate Questioning
7. Appropriate Uncertainty
8. Compliance with Rules
9. Appropriate Escalation
10. Safety Adherence
Step 13

13. Customer Feedback

Customer feedback is an integral quality signal. We examine ratings, written reviews, complaint tickets, refund requests, repeat interactions, and escalations.

Note: Customer ratings are one quality signal and are not treated as a standalone measure of clinical or legal competence.

Step 14

14. Peer Review

Selected answers are reviewed by other qualified professionals in the exact same domain. For example, a veterinary dermatology answer is audited by an experienced veterinary peer rather than a general customer service agent.

Peer review evaluates: Incorrect reasoning, missing critical warnings, communication clarity, scope violations, and opportunities for clinical improvement.

Step 15

15. Automated Quality & Safety Monitoring

Our algorithms continuously scan consultations to flag cases requiring immediate human audit:

  • Potentially dangerous medical or physical advice
  • Contradictory or incoherent recommendations
  • Consultations outside an expert's approved scope
  • Failure to recognize obvious emergency triage triggers
  • Unusual response times, answer copy-pasting, or anomalous patterns
Step 16

16. Manual Quality Review

When an answer or profile is flagged, our Compliance Committee steps in. Actions include: requesting formal clarification, requiring supplemental documentation, re-administering technical assessments, temporarily restricting categories, placing accounts under intensive probation, or permanent depaneling.

Step 17

17. Ongoing Credential Verification

Verification is not permanent. Professional licenses, certifications, and registrations expire or change. We periodically re-check primary regulatory registries.

Experts must renew credentials promptly. Failure to maintain active standing results in automated immediate suspension from answering questions in regulated categories.

Step 18

18. Expertise-Based Question Routing

Our verification taxonomy directly powers question distribution. When a customer asks:

"My dog has severe itching, hair loss and red skin. What could be causing it?"

The system classifies the query as Veterinary → Dog → Dermatology and routes it specifically to professionals with verified Dog Dermatology experience, rather than dispatching it to any general veterinarian.

Scope Standards & Enrollment Outcomes

Staying Within Your Verified Scope

Experts must operate strictly within their verified professional and specialty domain. A professional verified for Dogs → Dermatology must not represent themselves as a Veterinary Cardiologist unless that specialty has been separately audited and approved.

What If Your Expertise Changes?

As you gain new certifications, complete fellowships, or obtain new state licenses, you can request category expansion. Supplemental verification is conducted before new badges appear on your public profile.

Possible Enrollment Outcomes

✅ Approved

All identity, credential, and specialty requirements successfully audited. Platform consultation privileges activated.

⏳ Approved with Limited Expertise

Primary credentials verified, but specific specialty claims require additional documentation before activation.

📝 Additional Information Required

Non-fatal discrepancy or unconfirmed records requiring applicant clarification or document re-submission.

🔍 Manual Review

Complex institutional credentials or regulatory jurisdiction requirements undergoing secondary review.

❌ Not Approved

Failure to satisfy statutory eligibility, fraudulent documentation, revoked license, or serious regulatory sanctions.

What Can Cause Rejection or Restriction?

  • False identity or impersonation attempts
  • Use of another person's credentials or license number
  • Forged or materially altered documents
  • Misrepresentation of qualifications or degrees
  • Revoked, suspended, or restricted professional licenses
  • Materially false experience claims
  • Serious professional disciplinary records or malpractice findings
  • Repeated provision of advice outside verified scope

Applicant Readiness & Professional Responsibility

What You Should Have Ready Before Applying

1. Personal Information

Legal name, date of birth, contact details, official government photo ID.

2. Professional Information

License/registration number, issuing state/board, degree credentials, accredited certifications.

3. Experience Records

Employer history, clinic/firm affiliations, verified practice areas, years in active practice.

4. Specialty Documentation

Specialty board certifications, fellowship transcripts, continuing medical/legal education records.

How We Protect Legitimate Experts

Our objective is not to create unreasonable barriers for qualified professionals. A legitimate professional with genuine credentials should complete the process smoothly. We distinguish between routine baseline verification and additional investigations triggered by discrepancies.

Important Professional Responsibility

⚠️ Mandatory Safety Triage Duty

Experts remain individually responsible for providing advice consistent with applicable professional standards, statutory regulations, and the inherent limitations of remote advice. Experts must recognize when a user requires emergency, hands-on, or in-person evaluation (such as 911, ER, urgent hospital dispatch, or physical examination).

A Fair and Ongoing Standard

Verify the person • Verify the profession • Verify the expertise • Verify the safety judgment • Monitor the performance • Re-verify when necessary
